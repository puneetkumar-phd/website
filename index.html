Biography

Mechanistic insight across molecular and biomedical scales.

My research connects atomistic simulation with data-driven disease biology to identify therapeutic mechanisms, targets, biomarkers, and candidate molecules.

Protein dynamicsDrug discoveryAI/MLMulti-omics

I am a drug discovery researcher and computational biologist investigating how proteins change their three-dimensional conformations, recognise ligands, and participate in disease-associated molecular processes. My work focuses on the molecular determinants of protein function, ligand affinity and selectivity, conformational transitions, and therapeutic response, particularly in cancer, inflammation, and neurodegeneration.

I integrate GPU-accelerated molecular dynamics, enhanced sampling, molecular docking, virtual screening, alchemical and end-point free-energy calculations, QM/MM modelling, density functional theory, QSAR, and computational analysis of transcriptomic and multi-omics data. These approaches allow me to move between atomic-level mechanisms and systems-level biological signatures.

I am currently building and validating a reproducible GPU-enabled research environment centred on GROMACS, PLUMED, AMBER, Desmond, and the Schrödinger platform, together with cheminformatics and machine-learning workflows. The longer-term objective is to connect structural ensembles, simulation trajectories, chemical descriptors, biological networks, and omics signatures in integrated pipelines for target identification, compound prioritisation, and mechanism-informed lead optimisation.

Current development focusReproducible GPU workflows for molecular simulation, free-energy estimation, cheminformatics, machine learning, and multi-omics integration.
